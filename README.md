# Dead Names in a Frozen Archive: A Name-by-Name Re-identification of the Stranded Fonozoo Frog-Call Pile, With a Maintained-Archive Control

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24409230.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24409230)

Bioacoustics / data rescue · 2026

Author: Atharva Chourey
Affiliation: Atharva Chourey Lab (independent research lab)

*Manuscript — not yet peer-reviewed.*

## Abstract

Sound archives assembled before the molecular era are filed under the taxonomy of their day. When such an archive is abandoned — its files stranded on the web, its catalogue frozen — every subsequent taxonomic revision silently falsifies a fraction of its labels, and no one re-counts them. We recovered the complete stranded web pile of the Fonoteca Zoológica (MNCN, Madrid): 1,013 frog-call audio files published as web companions to printed sound guides, present in no formal archive. Of the 629 recordings filed under firm species names, **146 (23.2%) no longer carry that name** in the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy — predominantly genus recombinations (129 recordings), with 15 junior synonyms and 2 split elevations; a further 6 recordings (5 names) are split-ambiguous, their filed name now denoting a complex of two or more species. Joining the institution's own catalogue (3,618 Darwin Core records) resolved 4 of the 6 ambiguous recordings on geographic evidence. The burden is region-structured: Neotropical recordings changed at 30.7%, Palearctic recordings carry a further 16% split-ambiguity, and Malagasy recordings only 5.7%. A second ruler — the Catalogue of Life (ASW lineage) — agrees with the GBIF Backbone on 87.4% of names and leaves the aggregate rate nearly unchanged (24.2% vs 22.8%), yet genuinely disagrees on 18 names. As a control, we ran the identical instrument over the maintained Fonoteca Neotropical Jacques Vielliard (5,793 firm-named amphibian recordings): **1.1% changed**. The 23% figure is therefore not a property of an era but of a *filing state*: the measured dead-name burden of a frozen archive, against a living one.

*Keywords:* bioacoustics; sound archives; taxonomy; Fonoteca Zoológica; GBIF Backbone; data rescue; pre-registration

## Key results

- **23.2% dead-named** — 146 of 629 countable species-named recordings no longer carry their filed name in the GBIF Backbone (129 genus recombinations, 15 junior synonyms, 2 split elevations); 6 more are split-ambiguous. Headline recomputed from the project's classification summary during the publication build.
- **The control is twenty-fold lower** — the maintained FNJV archive, run through the identical instrument, changed at 1.1% of recordings (1.6% of names). Dead-name burden is a property of filing state, not era.
- **Region-structured** — Neotropical 30.7% changed (genus moves), Palearctic 16.0% changed plus 16.0% split-ambiguous (splits, not moves), Madagascar 5.7% (its guide postdates the region's genus reorganisation).
- **Two rulers disagree honestly** — GBIF and the Catalogue of Life agree on 87.4% of names; 18 genuine taxonomic disagreements (a whole genus, *Platypelis*, among them) and 47 matcher artefacts bound the error on any single-backbone re-identification.
- **The pile was stranded** — 1,013 audio files recovered from the Fonoteca's web surface, present in no formal archive; the audit is filenames, a backbone and a catalogue join — no re-listening.
